My wife and I went through the D-visa process last year. Yes, purchasing an apartment or a house fully satisfies the accommodation requirement for the retirement visa. You just need to present the title deed (notary act) when you apply at your home country's Bulgarian embassy. However, before you sign anything, read up on Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner: Step-by-Step Guide and Common Pitfalls because foreign ownership laws regarding land can be tricky depending on whether you are buying a house with a yard or just a standard apartment.

For the financial threshold, as non-EU retirees, you both need to prove you receive a regular pension from your home country. Currently, the minimum required amount is quite low—essentially the Bulgarian minimum wage per month, plus an extra 20% for a spouse. Realistically though, immigration likes to see a comfortable buffer in your bank accounts to ensure you won't become a burden on their social system.

One huge bureaucratic hurdle we faced was translating and legalizing all our documents. Every single paper—from our criminal background checks to our pension statements—needs an Apostille and must be translated into Bulgarian by a sworn translator approved by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Do not try to get translations done in your home country unless they have official Bulgarian certification.

The D-visa itself must be applied for from your country of origin; you cannot convert a tourist stay into a retirement D-visa once you are already inside Bulgaria. Once your D-visa is approved (which can take anywhere from 45 to 90 days), you travel to Bulgaria, rent or use your property, and then immediately apply for your temporary residence permit (lična karta) at the local migration office.

Medical insurance is another mandatory item they will grill you on. You have to purchase a private health insurance policy that meets specific Bulgarian migration requirements for the duration of your stay before you can get your final residency card, even if you plan to transition into the local public healthcare system later on.

A quick tip regarding the pension proof: make sure your official letters from your pension provider explicitly state the monthly gross and net amounts, and that the payments are lifelong. The migration officers in Bulgaria love official-looking seals and explicit wording, and any ambiguity will result in your application being sent back for corrections.
