Heating Costs in Bulgaria During Winter: Electricity vs Pellet Stoves in Older Homes

FrostByte7 OP
03.01.2025 09:26
Hey everyone, I recently moved into an older, poorly insulated brick house in provincial Bulgaria and I am trying to figure out the most cost-effective way to heat it this winter. My current options are either installing a pellet stove or sticking with traditional AC unit/radiator electric heating, but I am worried about the skyrocketing electricity and pellet prices. For those living in similar older homes, what are your average monthly bills looking like, and which system actually saves more money in the long run?

Living in an old brick house in provincial Bulgaria is tough during winter because the walls just suck the heat right out. I switched from ACs to a pellet stove two years ago, and while pellets aren't dirt cheap anymore, it is still much cozier. My monthly bills during peak winter hover around 350-400 leva for pellets, whereas my electricity was hitting nearly 600 leva trying to keep the place warm with standard radiators.
InfiniteBlue
13.09.2025 14:49
If you bought one of those fixer-uppers, definitely check out some guides on rural house renovation realities in Bulgaria to see what you are up against with insulation. Honestly, without external insulation (samisolyatsiya), electric ACs will run constantly and drive your bills through the roof. A pellet boiler with radiators is usually the better bet for older, drafty spaces.
EchoRider
09.11.2025 07:01
Inverters (high-end ACs) are way more efficient than regular electric heaters or oil radiators, but in an uninsulated old brick house when temperatures drop below minus 10 Celsius, their efficiency plummets. I use an AC for the shoulder months (October/November and April) and fire up the pellet stove from December through February. It gives the best balance.

Pellet prices have stabilized a bit compared to the crazy spike two years ago, but hauling bags all winter is a workout. You are looking at about 1.5 to 2 tons of pellets for a moderately sized old house over the winter season. Expect to pay around 700-900 leva total for the fuel depending on quality.

Stick with ACs if you hate physical labor. Pellets require cleaning the ash pan almost daily, scraping the burner pot, and stacking tons of bags. My neighbor has a pellet stove in his old brick house and curses every time he has to haul them up the stairs. My top-tier Daikin inverter keeps my living room warm, and my electric bill rarely exceeds 300 leva in January.

Honestly, wood burning (if you can source dry beech or oak) is still cheaper than pellets if you have a fireplace or a wood stove, but pellets are a great middle ground for cleanliness and automated feeding. Stay away from plain electric resistance heaters—they will bankrupt you in an uninsulated brick house.
