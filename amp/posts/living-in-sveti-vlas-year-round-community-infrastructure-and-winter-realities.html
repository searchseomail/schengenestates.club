We made the leap to live here year-round three years ago, and honestly, the winter vibe is completely different from July. Yes, it gets very quiet, but that is part of the charm if you want peace and quiet. For grocery shopping, the larger Janet supermarket near Sunny Beach stays open and is well-stocked, plus local smaller shops in Vlas remain operational. Just make sure your property has proper insulation because the Black Sea winds can get fierce in January.

Many year-round residents supplement with a fireplace or oil heaters. Also, make sure you double-check your paperwork—if you are buying a resale or off-plan, always review this guide on Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner: Step-by-Step Guide and Common Pitfalls to ensure your title deeds and utility connections are fully sorted for winter residency.

Medical services are a bit sparse right in Sveti Vlas during the off-season. There are local GPs, but for specialized care or major emergencies, most people head into Nessebar or Burgas. Burgas is only about 40 minutes away and has massive malls, great hospitals, and everything you need if you get cabin fever from the quiet winter streets.

Does it feel deserted? Between November and March, absolutely. The marina is practically a ghost town, and most restaurants close down. However, a core community of Brits, Russians, and locals still stick around. There are a few winter pubs and coffee spots where expats gather, so you won't be completely isolated if you make an effort to socialize.

One thing people forget is verifying the legal status of the building. If you are buying a second property for year-round living, remember that understanding documents like Act 14, Act 15, and Act 16 in Bulgaria Explained: Why You Must Never Skip Them is critical because buildings without Act 16 might have winter utility tariffs or maintenance issues that make year-round living a nightmare.

Public transport slows down significantly in the winter. The bus to Sunny Beach and Nessebar still runs, but much less frequently. If you live here year-round, having a car is practically mandatory unless you love walking long distances in the cold and rain.
