Maintenance Fee Disputes in Bulgarian Holiday Complexes: Rights, Laws, and Solutions

RootAccess
Topic Starter
Posted: 25.01.2025 06:44
I own an apartment in a coastal complex in Sunny Beach, and the management company has drastically increased the annual maintenance fees without proper justification or general assembly voting. When I questioned the sudden hike and requested a detailed breakdown of the expenses, they threatened to cut off my electricity and restrict access to the pool. What are my legal rights under Bulgarian property law, and how can owners effectively contest unfair fees and bullying tactics by developers?

This is a classic Sunny Beach management company scam. Cutting off electricity or restricting access to common areas like pools is completely illegal under Bulgarian law. They cannot take the law into their own hands, and you should report these coercive tactics to the police immediately.
StaticGhost
Replied: 16.07.2025 12:22
You need to check the Condominium Ownership Management Act (ZUPES in Bulgarian). Fees can only be legitimately increased if a General Assembly of owners was properly convened, a quorum was reached, and a valid vote passed with the required majority. If they bypassed this, the fee hike holds no legal weight.

Under Bulgarian law, utility cuts by private entities constitute a breach of your civil rights and potentially a criminal offense under the Penal Code for coercion. Do not let them bully you into paying unjustified amounts just because they hold the keys to the infrastructure.

You can deposit your legally owed maintenance fee (the original amount before the hike) into a notary-held escrow account or court account to prove you are not refusing to pay entirely, while your lawyer contests the illegal increase in court. This protects you from claims of default.
