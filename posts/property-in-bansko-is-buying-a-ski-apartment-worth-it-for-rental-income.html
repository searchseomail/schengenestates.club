Property in Bansko: Is Buying a Ski Apartment Worth It for Rental Income?

SavageSpark
Topic Starter
Posted: 03.01.2025 14:44
I've been looking at some surprisingly cheap apartments in Bansko lately and am seriously considering buying one as an investment for short-term holiday rentals. However, I keep hearing mixed reviews about the actual demand outside of the peak winter ski months and how high the maintenance fees can eat into profits. Does anyone here currently own property there, and is it realistic to expect a decent ROI, or are the summer months a total dead zone?

Replies (12)

LunarEclipse
Replied: 04.01.2025 13:30
I bought a one-bedroom in Bansko back in 2018, and honestly, the winter season is great, but summer is practically dead. Don't expect any meaningful rental income from May through October.
CyberNomad99
Replied: 25.02.2025 01:45
Before you pull the trigger, make sure you calculate all the hidden expenses. I strongly recommend reading this guide on Hidden Costs of Buying Real Estate in Bulgaria because the maintenance fees (or 'support tax') can really catch you off guard if the complex has a pool and spa.
UrbanOracle
Replied: 09.05.2025 05:41
The maintenance fees in some of the larger gated complexes are criminal. They charge 10 to 12 euros per square meter annually, whether your apartment is rented out or sitting empty. That eats right into your profit margins.
GlitchArtist
Replied: 01.06.2025 01:27
If you are a foreigner, navigating the legal side is crucial, especially if you are looking at land vs. apartments. Check out this overview on Can Foreigners Buy Land in Bulgaria to understand how ownership structures work here.
RogueVector
Replied: 14.06.2025 09:34
Demand in winter is definitely real, driven mostly by British, Romanian, and Greek tourists looking for cheap skiing. But peak season is short—really only January and February. December can be hit-or-miss depending on the snow.
GlitchArtist
Replied: 20.06.2025 01:14
Summer is definitely a dead zone for short-term holiday rentals, though there is a tiny niche of digital nomads and mountain bikers who show up in July and August. Still, don't budget for summer income.
AlphaProtocol
Replied: 28.06.2025 02:15
Make sure you factor in management company fees. If you aren't living in Bulgaria, you'll need someone to handle check-ins, cleaning, and repairs, and they usually take 20% to 30% of your gross rental revenue.
RetroRebel
Replied: 09.08.2025 11:32
For a complete walkthrough of the entire acquisition process, I found this Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner guide super helpful when I was doing my initial research.
VortexSurfer
Replied: 21.08.2025 10:58
Is it worth it? Only if you buy it primarily for your own use during ski season and treat the rental income as a bonus to help cover maintenance. If you're looking at it purely as a high-ROI investment vehicle, you'll likely be disappointed.
RogueVector
Replied: 30.08.2025 02:14
The cheap prices are alluring—seeing apartments for 25k to 30k euros makes your jaw drop. But remember why they are cheap: oversupply and high yearly maintenance fees that owners struggle to get rid of.
CyberNomad99
Replied: 21.12.2025 13:33
Property taxes and utility bills are very low in Bulgaria compared to Western Europe, which softens the blow during the summer months when the flat is sitting empty.
RetroRebel
Replied: 30.01.2026 14:58
If you do decide to buy, look for properties closer to the gondola station or in old town Bansko rather than the massive mega-complexes on the outskirts. The ones closer to the action have a much higher occupancy rate in the winter.
