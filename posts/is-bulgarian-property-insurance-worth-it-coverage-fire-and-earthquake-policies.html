Is Bulgarian Property Insurance Worth It: Coverage, Fire, and Earthquake Policies

PixelPioneer
Topic Starter
Posted: 28.01.2025 11:01
I recently purchased an apartment in Sofia and I'm debating whether to get comprehensive property insurance. Given the seismic activity in the region, earthquake coverage seems important, but I'm unsure if standard policies actually pay out or just have too many loopholes. Are fires and natural disasters adequately covered by local insurers, or is it a waste of money? I would love to hear experiences from other expats or locals who have had to make a claim.

Make sure you read the fine print regarding earthquake coverage. Some basic policies only kick in if the earthquake reaches a certain magnitude on the Richter scale, so paying a bit extra for comprehensive is smart.

I had a minor kitchen fire a couple of years ago. The insurance company sent an assessor within 48 hours and actually covered the repainting and kitchen cabinet repairs without too much fuss. Just keep all your receipts!
GlitchArtist

If you ever plan to rent it out or eventually sell your Bulgarian property remotely, having a clean, insured history with a reputable local provider makes buyers and property managers feel a lot more secure.
FrostByte7

Watch out for the under-insurance clause. If your apartment is worth 150,000 euros and you insure it for 80,000 to lower the premium, they will only pay a fraction of any claim proportionally. Insure it for its actual replacement value.
ByteStorm

Honestly, most local insurers like DZI or Bulstrad are reliable for standard claims like pipe bursts and fires. Earthquakes are rarer, but after the recent tremors in the Balkans, it's not worth skipping.

Many expats forget about liability insurance, which is usually bundled into these property policies. If your washing machine floods the apartment below you, that part of the policy literally saves your skin.
