Property Management Companies in Bulgaria: What Services Do They Provide and What Do They Charge?

NullPointer
Topic Starter
Posted: 13.02.2025 16:13
I recently purchased an apartment in Bansko and I'm looking for a reliable property management company to handle rentals and maintenance while I'm abroad. What specific services are typically included in their standard packages, and how much should I expect to pay in management fees and commissions? I'd love to hear from other foreign owners about their experiences and any hidden costs I should watch out for.

Congrats on the Bansko purchase! I've had an apartment near the Gondola for about four years now. A standard management package usually covers key holding, guest check-in/out, basic cleaning between stays, linen laundry, and utility bill payments. For ongoing maintenance like minor repairs, they usually charge an hourly rate plus the cost of materials. For rental management, most agencies charge between 20% to 30% of the gross rental income. If you just want a caretaker for when you're away without rentals, they usually charge a fixed annual fee ranging from 300 to 600 euros depending on the apartment size.

Be very careful with the so-called 'maintenance fees' charged by the complex developers or management companies themselves. Many foreign owners end up in bitter disputes over common area upkeep. I recommend checking out this guide on maintenance fee disputes in Bulgarian holiday complexes before signing anything. Some companies slap on exorbitant yearly maintenance fees for amenities like pools or elevators that barely work during the off-season.

To add to what others said about commissions, rental platforms like Booking and Airbnb take their cut first (around 15%), and then the local property management company takes their 25% of the remainder. So your net income can shrink quickly. Also watch out for hidden costs like 'advertising fees', portal syndication fees, or mandatory deep cleans once a year. Always get a detailed, itemized contract in both English and Bulgarian.

ShadowMatrix
Replied: 07.09.2025 16:26
My management company charges a flat 20% commission on all bookings they generate, but if a guest books through my own Airbnb listing and they just handle the check-in and cleaning, they charge a flat fee per turnover (about 40 leva for a one-bedroom). One hidden cost that caught me off guard initially was the annual municipal tourist tax declarations and utility account maintenance. Make sure your management company includes tax representation or at least reminds you when local Bansko municipality taxes are due.

Replied: 26.09.2025 14:12
Before settling in Bansko, I actually looked at properties all over the country, and the management dynamics vary wildly compared to rural areas. For instance, if you ever fancy a countryside retreat, read up on buying a country house in Veliko Tarnovo. But for Bansko ski apartments, the competition among management companies is fierce. Don't just go with the first agency recommended by the developer. Interview at least three, ask for references from other British or European owners, and make sure they have a physical office in Bansko so they can respond quickly to frozen pipes or broken heaters in winter.

Replied: 13.10.2025 13:42
A huge hidden cost to watch out for is winter utility bills. Even when your Bansko apartment is empty, the common area electricity, heating, and security fees accumulate. My management company deducts these monthly from a deposit I keep with them. Always maintain a float balance of at least 300-500 euros with your agency so they can pay bills on time without cutting off your electricity or internet.
OmicronDrift
Replied: 17.11.2025 09:35
Communication is the biggest pain point with Bansko property managers. Some are great for the first few months and then ghost you during the summer off-season. Make sure your contract includes a clause about monthly financial reporting and photo updates after cleanings or maintenance work. If they can't provide transparent invoices for repairs, walk away immediately.
