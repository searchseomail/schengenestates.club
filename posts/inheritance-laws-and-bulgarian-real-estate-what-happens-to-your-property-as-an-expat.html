Inheritance Laws and Bulgarian Real Estate: What Happens to Your Property as an Expat?

CosmicCoffee
Topic Starter
Posted: 02.02.2025 02:05
As a UK citizen who recently bought an apartment in Bansko, I am trying to figure out how Bulgarian inheritance laws apply to foreign property owners. If something happens to me, does my estate automatically follow Bulgarian forced heirship rules, or can I stipulate my wishes through a will made back home? I want to make sure my partner inherits the property without getting tangled in massive legal disputes or unexpected EU succession regulations. Any advice or experiences from fellow expats would be greatly appreciated.

First of all, welcome to Bansko! It is a lovely place, but you are smart to look into this early. Under EU Regulation 650/2012 (often called Brussels IV), the default rule is that the law of your habitual residence applies to your estate. However, as a UK citizen, you can explicitly choose for UK law to apply to your immovable property through a properly drafted will.
CaffeineFiend
Replied: 18.02.2025 03:47
Bulgaria does have forced heirship rules (Zakon za nasledstvoto), meaning a certain portion of your estate automatically goes to children or spouses. If you want to bypass this in favor of your partner, you need to be very careful. Since you are looking at long-term planning, you might also want to check out insights on the Bulgarian Property Market Forecast to see how values are trending for future generations.
AsyncGhost
Replied: 20.02.2025 13:25
If you and your partner are not legally married, you face an even bigger hurdle in Bulgaria. Unmarried partners have virtually no automatic inheritance rights under Bulgarian law. Even if you made a will back in the UK, local notaries and courts can make things complicated if forced heirship claims are made by blood relatives.

My advice is not to rely solely on a UK will. While Brussels IV allows you to elect your national law, you should have a local Bulgarian lawyer draft a bilingual will or officially register your intentions with the Bulgarian Chamber of Notaries. It saves your partner months of translation and apostille nightmare.

Be very careful with English wills dealing with foreign real estate. Bulgarian authorities heavily rely on notarized documents executed in front of a Bulgarian notary. A standard UK solicitor might not structure the clause for 'choice of law' under EU succession regulations correctly.

Replied: 07.08.2025 06:13
If you pass away without a clear local will, the Bulgarian courts will step in, and estranged relatives could theoretically claim their reserved portion under forced heirship. Don't leave it to chance—get a local specialist involved immediately.
MidnightGale
Replied: 16.09.2025 01:45
I bought a flat in Bansko back in 2018. When I asked my notary about inheritance, they strongly recommended making a 'handwritten will' (sakat zapaveshtanie) deposited with a Bulgarian notary, or explicitly stating in my UK will that it covers my Bulgarian assets under Brussels IV.
NebulaRider
Replied: 28.10.2025 03:58
The EU succession regulation actually makes things easier for expats *if* you use the opt-out clause correctly. Because the UK opted out of Brussels IV, UK nationals can still utilize the choice-of-law provision to apply English law, but proving that to a Bulgarian judge without local legal help is nearly impossible.
