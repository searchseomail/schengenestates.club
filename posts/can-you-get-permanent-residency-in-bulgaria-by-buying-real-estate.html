Can You Get Permanent Residency in Bulgaria by Buying Real Estate?

MidnightGale
Topic Starter
Posted: 07.01.2025 07:48
I am currently looking into moving to Europe and have been considering Bulgaria due to its affordable property market and beautiful scenery. However, there seems to be a lot of conflicting information online about whether simply purchasing a home there grants you residency or a pathway to citizenship. Can You Get Permanent Residency in Bulgaria by Buying Real Estate, or does it only qualify you for a temporary visa? I would love to hear from expats or legal experts who have navigated this process recently.

Replies (10)

VortexSurfer
Replied: 04.02.2025 11:39
To answer your question directly: No, simply buying real estate in Bulgaria does not automatically grant you permanent residency or citizenship. It only qualifies you to apply for a temporary residence permit (D visa), which you have to renew annually.
SilentVortex
Replied: 11.05.2025 06:56
I went through this exact process last year. You can get a temporary residence permit if you invest a certain amount, but it is definitely not a shortcut to permanent residency right away. Make sure you read a good Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner: Step-by-Step Guide and Common Pitfalls before putting down any deposits.
ByteStorm
Replied: 14.05.2025 10:37
Many agencies online mislead people into thinking property ownership equals instant residency. It doesn't. You get a long-term visa basis, but permanent residency requires living there continuously for several years.

Replied: 03.08.2025 06:01
Keep in mind that aside from the purchase price, you need to budget for ongoing costs. You should check out Annual Property Taxes and Maintenance Fees in Bulgaria: What Should You Expect to Pay? to avoid any nasty surprises after you buy.

Replied: 07.08.2025 03:35
My lawyer in Sofia explained that property ownership gives you the right to apply for a temporary residency status, but you have to maintain health insurance, show sufficient funds, and spend a certain amount of time in the country each year.

Replied: 09.10.2025 13:05
The threshold for the investment visa used to be lower, but it was raised a few years ago. Even with that investment, it is strictly a temporary residency pathway initially, not permanent residency.
SolarPhantom

Don't rely on blogs from 2015 for this. The laws have tightened up significantly. Buying a cheap apartment on the Black Sea coast does not mean you can just move there permanently without going through the proper visa renewals.
